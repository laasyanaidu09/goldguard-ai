# GoldGuard Problem Statement

## The Personal Wealth Gap in Gold Ownership

Physical gold remains one of the world's most trusted stores of value, especially in Asian, Middle Eastern, and Latin American households. Families accumulate gold jewelry over decades as heirloom assets, emergency savings, and symbols of celebration. 

However, managing this wealth presents critical challenges for the modern consumer:

1. **Information Fragmentation**: Gold assets are stored in physical safety boxes. Invoices are paper slips that fade or get lost. Owners do not know the exact gross weight, net gold weight, or purity mix of their collections.
2. **Hidden Valuation**: Because gold market rates fluctuate daily and jewelry contains manufacturing fees ("making charges"), owners have no clear, real-time estimate of what their jewelry is actually worth today.
3. **Underutilized Assets**: When planning a new jewelry purchase (e.g., for weddings or festivals), buyers rarely analyze whether they can exchange/recycle under-worn jewelry to offset costs.
4. **Redundancy and Impulsive Purchases**: Without a portfolio view, consumers buy similar designs repeatedly, missing opportunities to diversify styles (traditional vs. contemporary) or category mix (necklaces vs. bracelets).
5. **No Structured Saving Plans**: Planning for a large gold purchase is often speculative. Buyers do not have tools that analyze historical gold volatility to calculate realistic monthly or weekly savings goals under different price scenarios.
